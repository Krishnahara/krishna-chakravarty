Current: DTU BioNa & hard carbon anodes

Developing battery-grade hard carbon from lignocellulosic residues for sodium-ion batteries.

Precursor selection & fractionation

Kraft lignin, wheat straw, forestry residues — mapping chemical composition to carbon yield and electrochemical performance. DES extraction and LignoBoost precipitation protocols.

Pyrolysis & carbonization

Two-stage thermal conversion: 400–600°C pre-carbonization to fix structure, then 1000–1600°C hard carbon generation under inert atmosphere. Target: >0.38 nm interlayer spacing with optimised nanopore volume for Na+ storage.

Structural optimization

Surface passivation, nitrogen doping, and pore engineering to improve first-cycle coulombic efficiency and reversible capacity. Target: >300 mAh/g, ICE >85%.

Earlier research

PhD at DTU (2012–2016): thermodynamic modelling of ion-exchange processes for smart water EOR. DFT, Extended UNIQUAC, geochemical simulation.

Social science of innovation

The "why and how" of innovation — systems thinking, industrial strategy, and the organisational patterns that determine whether good science becomes real technology.

Industrial simplification as strategy

Industrial innovation often succeeds not by adding sophistication, but by removing failure modes. The containerised pyrolysis systems at MASH Makes were deliberately simpler than refinery-scale alternatives — fewer moving parts, standardised feedstock prep, remote diagnostics. This let a small team manage distributed production in low-infrastructure environments.

Sustenance-floor economics

What is the minimum viable technical and organisational structure that keeps a system running in resource-constrained settings? Underproduction and strategic patience: why some technologies fail not because they don't work, but because they are pushed to scale before the failure modes are understood.

The translation gap

How scientific results move (or don't move) from journal to pilot to product. The design of innovation ecosystems — why DTU Skylab works. The role of standards and certification in creating markets for novel materials. How carbon accounting frameworks shape technology choices.

Flagship results

Curated highlights. Problem → approach → result.

Marine biofuel from agricultural residue

Problem: No viable pathway to convert low-grade biomass into ISO 8217-compliant marine fuel. Pyrolysis oils had a ~100-year history of industrial failure.

Approach: Two-stage thermochemical platform with real-time process control, feedstock standardisation, and remote monitoring. Built R&D infrastructure and supply chain partnerships from scratch.

Result: Industrially validated fuel tested by MAN Energy Solutions and burned in vessel-scale trials with DS Norden and DFDS. ~USD 25M raised. Recognised by CMAC and FPTR.

MASH Makes | 2016–present
Off-grid cold storage for Indian agriculture

Problem: 30–40% post-harvest losses in India due to lack of rural cold chain. Diesel-powered storage is expensive and carbon-intensive.

Approach: Standalone renewable-energy-driven cold storage integrating biomass gasification with solar PV and absorption cooling. Consortium of DTU, Aalborg University, and Indian field partners.

Result: System demonstrated on-farm. Innovation Fund Denmark project completed (3.6M DKK). Published in Solar Energy and Energy journals.

Innovation Fund Denmark | 2019–2021
Hard carbon anode from lignocellulosic residue

Problem: EU has 100% import dependency for battery-grade graphite (mainly China). Na-ion batteries need hard carbon anodes — and the best precursors may be European forestry and agricultural residues.

Approach: Applying decade-long pyrolysis and biomass fractionation expertise to control carbon microstructure for electrochemical performance. Feedstock-to-anode pipeline with DTU and industrial partners.

Result: Early-stage. TRL 3–4 target. Consortium building for Horizon Europe and national funding.

DTU BioNa | 2025–present
AI-driven scientific workflow integrity

Problem: Generative AI tools for grant writing often sacrifice scientific accuracy for fluency, producing plausible but unverifiable outputs.

Approach: Co-developed agentic AI architectures with structured reasoning pipelines, validation layers, and feedback loops that preserve domain fidelity.

Result: 4 patents filed (WO/2025). Systems deployed for industrial R&D workflow automation.
